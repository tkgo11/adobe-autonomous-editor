# Workflow: Talking Head / Presenter-Led Edit

## Trigger

Use for one dominant speaker, webcam/on-camera explanations, commentary, lectures, creator videos, testimonials, or presenter-led corporate content.

## Default assumptions

Unless the brief overrides them:

- prioritize speech clarity and natural delivery over flashy effects;
- remove obvious mistakes, long dead air, false starts, repeated phrases, and distracting filler when removal does not alter meaning;
- preserve natural breaths and conversational cadence;
- hide disruptive jump cuts with b-roll, punch-ins, reframes, or motivated graphics;
- use restrained motion graphics;
- keep the speaker's eyes near a stable visual anchor and avoid excessive reframing.

## Analysis priorities

Identify:

- transcript with word-level timing;
- filler words, retakes, sentence restarts, repeated ideas;
- long pauses and dead air;
- strong/weak takes when several exist;
- face framing, eye-line, headroom, hand gestures;
- sections requiring b-roll or visual explanation;
- audio noise, room tone, plosives, clipping, inconsistent distance;
- semantic chapter boundaries.

## Edit plan

1. Build a clean dialogue spine from the strongest complete thoughts.
2. Remove unusable takes and technical dead space.
3. Apply J/L cuts when they improve continuity.
4. Use modest punch-ins/reframes to disguise only the most visible jump cuts.
5. Insert b-roll where it adds information or hides structural edits.
6. Add lower thirds/title cards only when useful.
7. Add captions if requested or if the destination strongly implies them.
8. Use background music sparingly and duck it under speech.

## Premiere rules

- Keep dialogue on dedicated dialogue tracks.
- Prefer text-based edit/transcript-derived timing where supported, but verify timeline results independently.
- Use adjustment layers for global treatment instead of duplicating effects across every cut.
- Stabilization is allowed only when it does not create objectionable crop/warping.
- Avoid automated zoom on every sentence or beat.

## After Effects escalation

Use AE for:

- branded openers/lower thirds;
- callouts following speaker gestures;
- advanced kinetic typography;
- screen inserts/replacements;
- tracked labels;
- complex cleanup or compositing.

Keep AE comps editable and parameterized.

## Audio

- establish consistent dialogue level and tone across cuts;
- patch hard cuts with room tone/crossfades;
- reduce noise conservatively to avoid metallic artifacts;
- duck music based on speech activity;
- verify consonant intelligibility on laptop/phone-like monitoring when possible.

## QC gates

Reject and repair if:

- a sentence changes meaning due to an edit;
- a jump cut is unnecessarily distracting;
- repeated automated punch-ins feel mechanical;
- lip/audio sync drifts;
- room tone changes abruptly;
- captions disagree with the edited speech;
- speaker framing clips face/hands unnaturally without stylistic intent.
