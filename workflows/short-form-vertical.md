# Workflow: Short-Form Vertical Social

## Trigger

Use for TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, vertical ads, or any short retention-focused social edit.

## Default assumptions

Unless specified:

- 9:16 delivery is preferred;
- derive the strongest self-contained hook early;
- remove setup that is not necessary to understand the payoff;
- use readable burned-in captions when dialogue is central;
- keep essential text/faces away from platform UI exclusion zones;
- maintain high information density without creating incomprehensible pacing.

## Analysis priorities

Score candidate segments for:

- hook strength in the opening seconds;
- self-contained meaning;
- novelty, emotion, surprise, utility, conflict, payoff;
- face/subject suitability for 9:16 crop;
- caption density and reading load;
- visually coverable jump cuts;
- likely loopable endings when appropriate.

## Edit plan

1. Select or construct a strong opening hook.
2. Compress setup aggressively while preserving comprehension.
3. Reframe every important shot for vertical delivery.
4. Use captions, b-roll, punch-ins, screenshots, callouts, or motion graphics to sustain clarity.
5. Remove redundant pauses and repeated concepts.
6. Preserve emphasis pauses that improve comedy, emotion, or comprehension.
7. End cleanly on payoff, CTA, or a deliberate loop.

## Premiere rules

- Build a dedicated vertical sequence rather than blindly cropping a landscape master.
- Use auto-reframe only as a starting point; have the agent inspect face/subject framing visually via rendered samples; never delegate that inspection to the user.
- Use nested sequences when deriving multiple aspect-ratio variants from one approved content cut.
- Keep a safe-zone guide layer or equivalent reference during graphics placement.

## After Effects escalation

Use AE for:

- kinetic captions beyond Premiere's reliable graphics control;
- animated emojis/callouts;
- tracked labels;
- dynamic split screens;
- stylized hooks and end cards;
- complex background extension or replacement.

## Caption policy

- segment by natural phrase, not arbitrary word count;
- avoid overwhelming the viewer with too many simultaneous words;
- emphasize only meaningful words;
- keep caption timing aligned to final audio, never pre-edit transcript timing.

## QC gates

Reject and repair if:

- hook starts with avoidable dead time;
- face/subject is cropped incorrectly;
- captions collide with platform UI or important visual content;
- cuts feel so dense that speech becomes hard to understand;
- graphics are too small on a phone-sized preview;
- the final frame ends accidentally rather than intentionally.
